# Literature matrix

Cutoff: 24 September 2026. “Evidence” is limited to what the cited primary source reports; results should not be read as independent reproduction.

| Human preference alignment | [InstructGPT / Ouyang et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155), [OpenAI summary](https://openai.com/index/instruction-following/) | Demonstrations → preference comparisons → reward model → PPO; human preference could favor a smaller aligned model over the larger base on evaluated prompts. | Preference is not truth. Preserve factuality and held-out capability checks. |
| Direct preference | [DPO (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290) | Derives a classification-style objective from a KL-regularized reward-maximization model under assumptions. | Easier offline baseline, but inherits dataset support, preference-model and reference-policy assumptions; not online exploration. |

## RLCD source audit

The official TypeSafe announcement is the primary source for the 15 September claim. Its public description supports only this narrow account: input state is mapped to typed structured decisions and probability-like outputs, and the company names the training approach RLCD. The official page itself clarifies that type locking prevents malformed or out-of-range outputs; it does not prove the selected answer is factual. In the materials reviewed, there is no algorithm specification comparable to a paper, no model weights or training code, and no independently reproducible benchmark protocol. Therefore: **established fact** = announcement and advertised interface exist; **not established** = an empirically superior method, universal calibration, or ability to replace RLHF/RLVR for generation.
